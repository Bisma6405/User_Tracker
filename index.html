Human–Computer Interaction

Simple notes and diagrams on how people interact with technology — and how to design interfaces that are easy, efficient and pleasant to use.

1. What is HCI?

Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people use computers and how interfaces can be designed to fit human abilities, needs and limits. It combines psychology, design and computer science.

Goals: make systems useful, usable and enjoyable; reduce errors; save time; include all kinds of users.

Key terms: UsabilityUser experience (UX)AffordanceFeedbackMental modelAccessibility

Why does HCI matter?

Poor interfaces cause mistakes, frustration and lost money. Good ones feel invisible — users reach their goal without thinking about the tool.

What is a mental model?

The picture in a user’s head of how a system works. Good design matches the user’s mental model, so behaviour is predictable.
